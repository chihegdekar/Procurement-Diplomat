# Post Idea \#1

# **Post Idea \#1**

**You don't need more leverage to negotiate better deals.**

I think we use leverage as a crutch.

*"Sure... I'd negotiate like that too if I had more buying power."*

No.

You need to see the negotiation differently.

Some of my favourite Knight's Moves:

Instead of bringing in expensive consultants to "fix the supplier relationship", we had the Head of Procurement quietly stand behind the stakeholder during the meeting.

The supplier's entire behaviour changed.

Result: A negotiation that had stalled for weeks suddenly moved forward because we solved an ego problem—not a commercial one.

---

Instead of accepting a finance target that everyone knew would damage the supplier relationship, we changed the internal conversation before we changed the external one.

Cost: One 20-minute pre-brief.

Result: Better alignment, a stronger supplier relationship, and a deal nobody had to apologise for six months later.

---

Instead of trying to "win" another price negotiation, we mapped what every stakeholder was actually trying to protect.

Cost: A sheet of paper and a whiteboard.

Result: Millions protected, a supplier that wanted to keep working with us, and a procurement team that became more trusted inside the business.

---

**The biggest commercial breakthroughs rarely come from bigger budgets. They come from seeing what everyone else misses.**

You can find creative ways to unlock value without more leverage, more authority or a bigger budget.

Ask yourself:

**"What is everyone trying to protect?"**

Then ask:

**"What's the Knight's Move that changes the game instead of fighting harder inside it?"**

That's the fun part of negotiation to me.

Give me a monopolistic supplier.

Give me an impossible stakeholder.

Give me an unrealistic finance target.

We're going to find the bigger pie either way.

**"You can never solve a problem on the level on which it was created."** — Albert Einstein

That's a much stronger narrative than "doing more with less."

# **Post Idea \#2**

I actually think we should throw away about half of this template.

It's very "self-improvement Twitter."

Your audience doesn't respond to "grind harder."

They respond to **challenging conventional procurement wisdom**.

So let's rewrite it in your voice.

---

**Everyone wants better supplier deals.**

Nobody wants to do what actually creates them.

And I have bad news.

It isn't another negotiation tactic.

It's better preparation.

---

Most procurement teams believe negotiations are won in the meeting.

They're not.

They're won long before anyone walks into the room.

By the time the first offer is made, the outcome is usually heading in one direction already.

---

If your stakeholders aren't aligned before the meeting...

You're probably not going to get the deal you hoped for.

---

Then there's the question almost nobody asks.

**What is everyone actually trying to protect?**

Because until you know that...

You're negotiating symptoms.

Not causes.

---

I've watched hundreds of negotiations across automotive, manufacturing and enterprise procurement.

The biggest breakthroughs almost never came from a clever line or a stronger BATNA.

They came from understanding the commercial, political and emotional pressures driving every person at the table.

---

The logic is simple.

People don't negotiate from spreadsheets.

They negotiate from pressure.

Pressure shapes behaviour.

Behaviour shapes agreements.

Agreements shape commercial outcomes.

---

Read that again.

**People don't negotiate positions. They negotiate pressures.**

---

And finally, dear Procurement Directors...

Winning the negotiation might not be the victory you think it is.

I've seen organisations celebrate a 4% price reduction...

...only to spend the next 18 months paying for it through expedites, supplier churn, quality issues and damaged relationships.

The savings didn't disappear.

They simply came back wearing a different name.

---

So stop chasing better negotiations.

Build a procurement team that sees further than everyone else.

Because procurement doesn't exist to cut costs.

**It exists to prevent commercial regret.**

---

After helping negotiate hundreds of millions of dollars in commercial agreements, I can tell you the difference between the teams that constantly firefight... and the ones everyone trusts with the biggest deals comes down to just **three battlefields**.

Every Procurement Diplomat learns to master:

* **Preparation** *(Clarity)*  
* **People** *(Psychology)*  
* **Politics** *(Alignment)*

Miss even one, and you'll spend the negotiation reacting instead of leading.

---

### **1\. Preparation**

Most procurement teams prepare for the negotiation.

Very few prepare the organisation.

Engineering has one objective.

Finance has another.

Operations has another.

Sales promises something procurement doesn't know about.

Then everyone wonders why the negotiation falls apart.

By the time you walk into the supplier meeting, you've already inherited internal conflict.

Get preparation right?

The negotiation becomes a confirmation of decisions you've already aligned—not a battle to fix problems live.

---

### **2\. People**

Most negotiators focus on positions.

Elite negotiators focus on pressures.

Every difficult supplier.

Every impossible stakeholder.

Every aggressive executive.

Every unreasonable demand.

Is usually protecting something.

Status.

Certainty.

Recognition.

Security.

Control.

The moment you identify what someone is protecting, their behaviour starts making sense.

That's when you stop reacting to personalities and start influencing outcomes.

---

### **3\. Politics**

Most procurement teams think they're negotiating with suppliers.

They're not.

They're negotiating with their own organisation.

Finance.

Engineering.

Legal.

Operations.

Sales.

Leadership.

The supplier is often the easy part.

The best Procurement Diplomats don't fight politics.

They align incentives.

When everyone wins together, supplier negotiations become dramatically simpler.

---

Negotiation isn't about becoming more persuasive.

It's about seeing more than everyone else at the table.

That's why Procurement Diplomats consistently create better commercial outcomes—they're playing three games while everyone else is playing one.

**Which of these three battlefields causes your team the most trouble: Preparation, People, or Politics?**

Today, the need for this capability is even greater.

Why?

Because when I first started...

Very few organisations realised:

* Supplier behaviour is driven as much by psychology as price.  
* Internal politics can destroy a negotiation before the supplier even joins the meeting.  
* The biggest commercial risks are usually created long before the negotiation starts.

Today?

Most Procurement Directors already know that if they're constantly firefighting, dealing with executive bypass, or inheriting supplier problems...

They don't have a negotiation problem.

They have a visibility problem.

They need a system that helps them see what everyone else misses.

Because once basic negotiation skills become table stakes...

The real advantage comes from understanding the commercial, political and human dynamics behind every decision.

The best procurement teams I've worked with aren't obsessed with tactics.

They're obsessed with diagnosis.

They know that the quality of their analysis determines the quality of their agreements.

Because they can see what everyone else is reacting to.

Those aren't buzzwords.

They're mental models that help procurement teams consistently make better commercial decisions.

The organisations that embrace them don't just negotiate better deals.

They become far harder to bypass, far more trusted internally, and far more influential in shaping commercial outcomes.

Because procurement doesn't create value by arguing harder.
