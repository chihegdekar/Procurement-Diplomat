why this is not a course

6 outcomes your next supplier meeting needs.

You have probably done the training and run the simulations. But neither can attend your supplier escalation meeting. This can.

01

Make them prove the increase

Separate a real cost change from a vague claim. See what is temporary, what is pass-through, and what does not hold up.

02

Stop a “one-off” becoming the baseline

A concession today can become the number every supplier uses tomorrow. Protect the precedent before it starts costing you.

03

Get something back when you move

If you give on price, get value back. Capacity. Better terms. Cost visibility. A future reduction. Supply protection.

04

Keep unpriced promises off the table

Find out what Finance, Engineering, Operations, and Legal can really move before someone gives it away in the meeting.

05

Protect the programme, not just the unit price

A price win that risks delivery, quality, or production is not a win. Make the trade-offs clear before you commit.

06

Walk in with an answer your leaders can defend

Know what you can accept, what you must refuse, and why. No scrambling after the supplier asks for a decision.

Objections

The questions everyone asks first

If yours isn’t here, email it. Ruth answers these herself.

chi@castlenegotiate.com
We already have experienced negotiators.

Good. Experience is not the question. The question is whether finance, engineering, operations and legal are aligned around one mandate before the supplier tests it. That’s where most experienced teams still lose value, and it’s the first thing we look at.

We do not have time for training right now.

This isn’t on a training calendar. It starts with the negotiation already on yours. The first step is 30 minutes about your live deal. If your negotiation is in 3 weeks, that’s exactly the right time to have this conversation, not the wrong one.

We cannot share confidential supplier information.

You don’t need to. We never ask for contracts, quotes, cost models or supplier names on the first call. You describe the shape of the situation. Everything after that happens under whatever confidentiality terms your legal team requires.

How is this different from Scotwork, CIPS or our consulting partner?

Classroom providers teach a method on a fictional case. Big consulting builds a cost model over 6 months. Both are useful. Neither can attend your escalation meeting on the 14th. We work on your live deal, we align your own functions first, and we leave the method behind so you don’t need us next quarter.

You will not understand our category.

Correct, and we don’t pretend to. You are the category expert. Our work is to pull your commercial, technical and political reality out of your team and turn it into a position. Ruth has done this across metals, packaging, castings, logistics, compressors, electronics and IT, in the US, Europe and Asia.

What does it cost?

The triage call costs nothing. Anything beyond it is scoped in discovery, priced against what’s at risk in your deal, not per seat and not per training day. You’ll get a number before you commit to anything, and you’ll know exactly what it’s buying.

One last thing

The answer you give this supplier sets the price of the next 5 asks.

One concession becomes the benchmark. Suppliers talk. Your own stakeholders remember what was allowed last time. The 8% is not the biggest risk in this negotiation. The precedent is.
